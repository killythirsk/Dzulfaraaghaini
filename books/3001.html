Principal Subjects

    Malik ibn Sabir

    Shepherd of the Banu Kathir · four goats, a threadbare tent and nothing else · first owner of the dinar · held two years for a murder no one asked him about · early-to-mid twenties by his sheet

    Shares his last meal with a stranger, is given a coin he can neither keep nor lose, and spends two years in another clan's custody without anyone asking where he was.

    Read the full entry

    Amina bint Rashid

    Daughter of the Banu Harith · Malik's wife and the dinar's second owner · twenty-two when she finds the coin answers to her, twenty-three by her sheet · murdered on the twenty-sixth of Deyren

    Learns the coin will answer to her for exactly as long as she wants it to, spends eleven months pricing a way to the sea, and is murdered by a stranger who has never heard her name.

    Read the full entry

    Sulayman ibn Ghanim

    Leader of the Banu Harith for nineteen years · Amina's uncle, her father's own brother · orders Malik's seizure while doubting the case · about fifty-eight to sixty-two by his sheet

    Doubts the case against Malik from the first night, gives the order anyway because his own nephews are already riding, and spends two years failing to act on what he suspects.

    Read the full entry

    Nur

    Sold into a river-city wine-house at fourteen · the dinar's fourth owner · later a widow of independent means three provinces from anywhere · late twenties by her sheet

    Listens to a dying man's whole confession without comment, takes the coin from his body, buys back her own debt, and waits two years for someone who wants the truth badly enough to be honest about why.

    Read the full entry

    Qasim al-Fahmi

    Scholar · the dinar's fifth owner · keeper of a catalogue of pressed desert wildflowers since boyhood · twenty-seven to twenty-nine by his sheet

    Meets a miracle with an inventory, spends five years following one coin through five hands, sends the truth north the night he learns it, and then puts the coin beyond any hand's reach.

    Read the full entry

    The Dinar

    Catalyst, Case 3,001 · a single struck gold coin · one face worn smooth · five holders, five transfers

    Returns to its latest legitimate owner the morning after it is spent, changes hands only by a witnessed word or an owner's death, and is ended by a scholar who breaks it past recognition.
