Three arrows not asked

At midnight in the month of Garren, in the thirtieth year, Levan of Kharzeni, Shalva of Tebukhi and Archil of Zemoli put three arrows to the fire of the forge at Kharzeni: “Let it be the one thing in this valley,” says Levan, “that was never asked for anything.” They will not burn for hours; it takes the best bellows in Vashkeni and the seven hours until dawn, and the seeds in the nocks go out without a flare, the way a lamp goes out when someone has simply closed the door of the room.

Subject Levan of Kharzeni, Shalva, Archil / Archive Case 0233 · Only a Little

In a dark forge lit by a bed of glowing coals, three old men stand shoulder to shoulder and each holds the tip of a long thin arrow, its grey fletching still on, in the embers: a white-haired smith in a leather apron with scarred, reddened forearms at the left, a bearded man in a shaggy fur mantle in the middle, and a white-bearded man in a dark embroidered robe with a ring on his finger at the right; an anvil stands at the lower left and a water barrel at the lower right.
