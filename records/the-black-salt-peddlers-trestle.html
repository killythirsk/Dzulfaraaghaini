The Black-Salt Peddler's trestle

At grey dawn in the Low Market the Black-Salt Peddler loosens the thong of a linen sack, and Ansbert draws one thick forefinger through the dull black powder and leaves a hair-thin silver line along the trestle's edge; the hawker's donkey sleeps on its feet behind the peddler. Ansbert pays nine pennies, the month's stall-rent, for the sack.

A middling man in a brown cap tips the mouth of a linen sack toward a thick-set man in a brown coat over a plank trestle laid with cakes of red ochre and small pots, the thick-set man drawing a finger through a heap of dark powder, a small grey donkey standing asleep behind them beside a stone horse-trough, stallholders waiting in the mud and a pale city wall under a grey sky.
