Bianca

Goat, called Provision Seven in the Order’s inventory · the goat that ate the writ · 4 on the sheet

The goat that ate the writ is a brown nanny of no particular distinction, called Bianca by the family that owned her and Provision Seven by the Order’s inventory. She does it on the fourth afternoon of the river, in the fifth boat, with the unhurried thoroughness of an animal that has understood that the paper in question is both unguarded and made of rag. It is the Order’s own copy, sealed and countersigned, the single document that licenses the removal of twelve hundred persons across a river, and the last of its eleven pages goes down in under a minute. Ottone, dozing against a barrel, wakes to find her regarding him with an expression of mild administrative satisfaction. He restores the pages from memory that evening, with the goat tethered at a punitive distance. She does not appear again.

The manuscript gives her colour and her two names, and no age, size or markings. The sheet’s age of 4, height of about 60 cm at the shoulder, narrow face with a white blaze, white sock, amber eyes, small beard, brass tag on a rope collar and pink skin are its own, as are its panels Content, Curious, Indifferent and Chewing and its view Eating the Writ. Its scene She eats the sealed writ on the fourth afternoon of the river. condenses the manuscript, and draws her on a stony track before a ruined arch, with a sealed envelope in her mouth; the manuscript puts her in the fifth boat and the writ in eleven pages.
