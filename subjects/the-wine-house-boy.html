The Wine-House Boy

The wine-house's own boy · unnamed · finds Zaid dead in his rented room · thirteen by the sheet

The river-city wine-house's own boy, who finds Zaid dead in his rented room. It is the discovery that ends the thief's three months of excess, and within the hour Nur has searched the body and taken the dinar. He belongs to the house of the owner who holds Nur's bond. The manuscript gives him no name.

The sheet draws a thin boy of about 150 cm, with a sharp, narrow face, short black curls, brown eyes and tan skin, the tip of one earlobe missing, in a grey apron over an off-white short tunic and sandals, with a brass tray of clay cups. Its expression panels run worried, fearful, grief-stricken and determined, and its last scene, Finds Zaid dead in his rented room, shows him kneeling over the body with the tray fallen beside it. The age of thirteen and everything visual are the sheet's.
