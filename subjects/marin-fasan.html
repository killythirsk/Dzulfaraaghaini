Marin Fasan

Foreman of the Duke’s southern salt pans · paid by the share · 50

The foreman of the Duke’s southern pans, which lie along the lagoon’s southern shore in a grid of five hundred shallow brown squares, is a squat, bandy, red-faced man of fifty with a voice like a wet rope and the habit of tasting everything he was shown. The Duke pays his rakers by the heap and his foreman by the share. In the first week of Ferren Marin takes on a foreigner who has walked up to the pan-sheds in a cold rain and asked, in an accent that made two of the rakers laugh, whether there was work for a man who knew how to hold a rake; being handed one, the man holds it correctly, and It is not a thing that can be faked. The pans pay in salt, so many measures a week in a canvas bag, and Marin, who finds him baffling, never learns why the arrangement suits him. His wife is Pierina.

The manuscript gives his age (fifty), his squatness, bandy legs, red face, voice and habit of tasting, and no height or hair. The sheet’s build of about 1.64 m, broad face, dark eyes, grey-black curls, grey stubble, wind-roughened skin, salt-stiff canvas smock, brown breeches and wooden rake are its own, as are its panels Pride, Weariness, Anger and Wry Smile and its views At the Salt Pans (Working), with a windmill and a tower beyond. Its scene Pays his rakers by the heap., in which he holds out an open palm to a young man in a hooded cloak, differs from the manuscript, in which the Duke pays the rakers by the heap and Marin is paid by the share. Its title, Foreman of the Duke’s salt pans, follows the manuscript.
