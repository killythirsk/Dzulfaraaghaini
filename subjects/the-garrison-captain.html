The Garrison Captain

Garrison captain sent by a governor two provinces removed · unnamed · starves both sides of grain until a truce is sworn, then orders Malik released · mid-forties by the sheet

The captain the governor dispatches to restore the peace of the roads, who does it by starving both sides of grain until a truce is sworn: an akad taken before his own officers, in place of any god or elder either side still fully trusted. When Qasim's sworn account reaches him by a paid courier, his clerks confirm what they can of it against a provincial scholars' registry, and he orders Malik released, without apology or compensation. The manuscript gives him no name.

The sheet draws him compact and upright, with a square jaw, a level mouth, hooded dark brown eyes, a short neat black beard, black hair greying at the temples under an iron-banded cap, olive skin and a pale scar across the left eyebrow, in a quilted dark-green tunic and a leather belt with brass fittings, with a straight sword at his hip and a wax-sealed dispatch case at his saddle. Its expression panels run authority (measured), suspicion (guarded), resolve (unyielding) and judgment (decisive), and its last scene is captioned he starves both sides of grain until a truce is sworn. The age of about forty-five, the scar and everything visual are the sheet's.
