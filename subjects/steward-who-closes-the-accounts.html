The Steward Who Closes the Accounts

Steward of Jakuen-ji · keeper of the ledger room · tells Isamu the debt is settled · 46 by his sheet

A steward of Jakuen-ji and keeper of its ledger room. Isamu asks him where Chiyo and Kiyo are and what six months means, and he tells him The debt is settled and turns back to the ledger room, the line the site files this case under. He decides, on the sum still owed, to take Kiyo as well. By the time he reaches his desk he has forgotten Isamu’s name, a professional skill his office taught him. He has a daughter in the lowland temple holdings, whom he never connects to the ledger room.

The pack treats him as a different man from the steward who announces the extension (39 by that sheet), but the manuscript does not say whether the two are one man or two, and it leaves open which steward the junior brother fetches.

His age and the way he looks are the pack’s. The sheet makes him medium height, about 5 ft 5 in (165 cm), neat, with a smooth, composed, plain face, a pleasant mouth, neutral brown eyes, pale skin and a shaved head, in a plain grey robe with a dark indigo sash, a ledger book at his hip and a ledger brush in his hand. Its expression panels are Composed, Thoughtful, Polite and Detached. Its scenes are the full figure front and back, and one captioned Turns back to the ledger room, with him seen from behind in a temple doorway. It titles him “Steward of Jakuen-ji”, as it does the other steward.

“The debt is settled.”
