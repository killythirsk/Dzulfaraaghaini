The Old Bow-Tender

Old bow-tender of the Hall of the Unstrung Bow · master to Rati · unnamed · 71 on his sheet

He has one sentence in the manuscript, reported and unnamed. The tender of the bow in the Hall of the Unstrung Bow has an apprentice, Rati, who has counted the ridges of the grip forty times (twenty-one one way, thirty-four the other), and who stopped trying to explain it to anyone after the old bow-tender told him that numbers were not a thing a boy ought to expect to come out meaning something.

He is, so far as the manuscript says, wrong. At the first, in Ormeli, Ketevan counts the bands laminated into the limb (three, five, eight, thirteen, twenty-one, each the sum of the two before it) and thinks of the unmistakable smell of an arrangement; the Archival Log records the bands and the ridges as present so that a sufficiently rigorous investigator might infer non-natural origin unassisted. The manuscript does not say whether the old man had counted them himself, what he knew, or what became of him.

The manuscript gives his post, his apprentice and his remark, and no age, name or face. The sheet’s age of 71, stooped, wiry build of about 5 ft 7 in, lined, sceptical, thin-lipped face, white hair under a grey wool cap, brown eyes, grey linen robe, brown leather accessories and small flask are its own, as are its panels Skeptical, Wearied, Quietly Concerned and Resolute and its scenes Wiping the Bow and Telling Rati that numbers are not a thing a boy should expect to mean something. The sheet’s quotation is its own wording of what the manuscript reports.
