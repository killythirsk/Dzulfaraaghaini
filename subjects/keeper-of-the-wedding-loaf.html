The Keeper of the Wedding-Loaf

Keeper of the Vigil · 45 years old

The keeper on duty the one day the Anchorite declines the day's bread. A ceremonial wedding-loaf has been baked and distributed to the whole quarter for a local festival, and the keepers have thought it a small kindness to include her. She refuses it outright and without explanation, in a woman who otherwise never refuses anything in eleven years, and the keeper never offers her that bread again, nor tells his successors quite why.

The sheet gives his age as 45 and draws him round-shouldered, about 5'8", with brown hair going grey, hazel eyes, a short beard and fair skin, with strong, work-worn hands and a flour-dusted sleeve, in a grey wool robe with a beige knotted cord, holding a festival loaf. Its expressions are kind, apologetic, sorrowful and resolute. Its last panel, captioned that she declines the wedding-loaf, the only refusal in eleven years, draws a young woman in a white hooded cloak standing before him among the tents of an open-air festival, where the manuscript has the Anchorite enclosed in her cell. The manuscript gives him no description.
