Ardenwake Children

Children of the holy city · approx. 4–12 years old

The children of Ardenwake, the holy city of the Vigil of Ardwen. They grow up beside the daily Empty Hand, a failed attempt on the sword, and learn the failed Attempter's walk down the cathedral steps before they learn their letters. In the square they perform it for each other as the Nothing Face, the slack look every failed Attempter wears on the way out, copied the way a child copies a blacksmith's hammer-swing or a fisherman's cast. The manuscript says it is never mockery: the children have simply noticed what the adults have grown too used to seeing. They also find the bread slot in the cathedral's north wall before their parents explain it, and ask the Anchorite whether she is cold, whether she has a name and whether she misses the sun. During the eleventh siege they gather at the wall and throw stones at siege towers they could not have dented with a mason's hammer, because their fathers are throwing stones beside them.

The sheet gives their ages as approximately 4 to 12 and draws them small, quick and mud-kneed, with uncombed flax-to-brown hair, bright grey and brown eyes, wind-pinked fair skin and patched undyed wool smocks, bare or rag-wrapped feet, sticks and small stones. Its expressions are pensive, defiant, watchful and weary, and its panels show the stone-throwing and the children at the bread slot. The manuscript gives them no physical description; those details are the sheet's.
