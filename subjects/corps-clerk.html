Corps Clerk

Clerk of the Corps · reviewer of the closing file · unnamed · 44 on his sheet

He appears in the last paragraph of the manuscript, after the histories and the Chronicle and the pilgrims’ plain arrows and the archivist’s empty room: On the Uncounted Day of the thirty-third year, a clerk of the Corps who had never heard of the Vashkeni basin reviewed the status line of a receiver in a closing file. It read standing by. He initialed it. Then, alone on its line, Filed.

The file he closes is the one that Observer 506 kept in four Notes, request by request, and the Custodian Diagnostic Log that follows them: thirteen arrows accounted for, ten loosed and three destroyed unfired, the Receiver in good order and standing by, no further request possible, Status: CLOSED. The manuscript gives the clerk no name, age, face or voice and does not say what the Corps is beyond that. It does not say that he read the Closing Note, which holds the finding. The status line is the only thing he reviews, and it is true.

The sheet’s age of 44, ordinary lean build of about 5 ft 8 in, short dark hair, dark brown eyes, grey tunic with a dark grey collar and belt, stylus and hand-held status panel are its own, as are its panels Neutral, Tired, Focused and Resolute. Its scene, He initials the closing file and moves on, follows the manuscript.
