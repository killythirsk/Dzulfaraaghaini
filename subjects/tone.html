Tone

Herd-boy, a herdsman’s son · Berto’s cousin · 12

In the first week of that autumn a herdsman’s son of twelve named Tone and his cousin Berto, who is nine, follow a strayed goat over the pass, look down into a valley full of lamps, hear something at dusk from the ridge that neither of them is prepared to describe, and run north until they fall over the woodpile of a shack that nobody remembers. The pale hermit who lives there gives them hot water and salted shrimp and listens while they tell him the whole of it: the lamp-people, the graveyard, the gray man with the shovel who did not flinch, the flower and the noise. He asks them one question, whether the river down there has a bend. Tone, who could see no possible reason for anyone to ask, says yes, a big one, at the far end, past the flower, round like a fist. They sleep by his fire and leave at dawn, remembering a fire, something salt and the feeling of having been looked after by no one, which their mother ascribes to fever. Years later the hermit counts it as the first time he was told of the bend.

The manuscript gives his age (twelve), his father’s trade and his answer, and no height, build, face or hair. The sheet’s small build of about 1.45 m, round cheerful face, sandy hair, blue eyes, freckles, gap in the teeth, undyed wool smock, crook and patched boots are its own, as are its panels Happy, Curious, Worried and Determined and its view At the Shack. Its title line, following a strayed goat over the pass and finding a shack by a fire, condenses the manuscript. Its scene Finds a shack by a fire draws a plank shack with a firelit door and a white goat by the fire; the manuscript’s shack is river-stone and split larch, and the goat is not at it.
