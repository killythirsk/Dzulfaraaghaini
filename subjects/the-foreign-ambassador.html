The Foreign Ambassador

Ambassador of an unnamed foreign court · 50 years old

Sent by a court that found the whole legend faintly ridiculous, he tries the sword in full view of his own retinue in order to carry home a story that would end the rumor for good, in the spirit of a man debunking a conjuring trick. He does not lift it, which surprises him less than what failing does: for the whole walk back to his delegation it leaves him feeling that something had actually been asked of him and found wanting, rather than proven, as he expected, to be nothing at all. He writes his official report exactly as his court wanted it written, and never fully believes his own report again. His court is never named.

The sheet gives his age as 50 and draws him spare and upright, about 5'11", with a long clean-shaven face and a dry half-smile, grey-streaked brown hair to the collar, hazel eyes, fair sallow skin, an ink stain on the right middle finger and a signet worn on a chain, in a plum wool dalmatic with embroidered cuffs under a charcoal travelling cloak, with a ceremonial dagger he never draws. Its expressions are pride, contemplation, dry amusement and disillusionment, and its last panel is the failed test and the report that followed. Since the manuscript names no court, the sheet's culture line, Frankish (Vosmark), is the sheet's placeholder.
