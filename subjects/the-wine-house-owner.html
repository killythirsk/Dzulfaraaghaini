The Wine-House Owner

Owner of the river-city wine-house that held Nur's bond · unnamed · weighs the coin once, counts it twice, and asks no questions · fifty-five by the sheet

The owner of the river-city wine-house that held Nur's bond. When she walks into his counting-room with the dinar and buys back her own bond, which thirty years of ordinary wages could not have touched, he weighs the coin once, counts it twice and asks no questions. His silence belongs with that of Hammad and of the caravan's chief factor: careful men, each touched by the same coin, none of them saying so aloud. The manuscript gives him no name, age or description.

The sheet draws him heavy and flat-faced, shrewd, with medium-olive skin, dark brown eyes, dyed black hair under a plum velvet cap and an oiled black moustache, with gold rings on the left hand, in a plum velvet robe with gold brocade trim and sash. Its expression panels run skeptical, thoughtful, irritated and calculating; its scenes are captioned At His Wine-House, Counting the Dinar, which shows a balance and coins on the table, and A Quiet Transaction at the Wine-House, and its pull-quote follows the character list's wording. The age of fifty-five and everything visual are the sheet's.
