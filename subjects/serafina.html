Serafina

Glazier’s widow of the mainland terraces · two sons buried in the war’s first winter · among the famine’s sixty-one thousand nine hundred · 58 on the sheet

She is one of the sixty-one thousand nine hundred whom the famine takes: a glazier’s widow of the mainland terraces named Serafina, who had buried two sons in the war’s first winter. She sells, pane by pane, every piece of colored glass her husband ever cut for her, to buy oil that has by then stopped being sold at any price. A neighbor who comes in the spring to borrow a lamp finds her sitting upright at her own table with the last unsold pane held to the window, turning the thin winter light through it, for no one, in every color it still knows how to make.

The manuscript gives her as a widow and a mother of two dead sons, and no age, height, build, face, hair or eye colour. The sheet’s age of 58, short heavy build of about 1.55 m, round tired face, grey hair under a scarf, brown eyes, sun-lined skin, nicked hands, dark wool dress and kerchief are its own, as are its panels Pensive, Tired, Resolute and Sorrow and its view Selling glass at the market. Its scene At the graves of her two sons (First winter of the war), in which a hooded figure crouches by a wooden cross and white flowers before a walled hill town, is not in the manuscript, which gives the burial only as a clause. Its title line, that she sells every piece of coloured glass her husband cut, to buy oil, follows the manuscript.
