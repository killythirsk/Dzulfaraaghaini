The Lit Ring

Sacred Doctrine of the Decree of Luminescence · one hundred and six iron posts, forty paces apart · closed on the third dusk at Val Muta

The Lit Ring is the doctrine that any settlement wishing to stand upon favored ground must bound it with lamps, one every forty paces, kindled at dusk from the parish’s Hearth-Lamp and kept burning until dawn. Everything within (the well, the granary, the graveyard, the cradle) lies under the Sun’s regard; everything beyond is merely ground, unaccounted for and not evil. At Val Muta it is closed on the third dusk: one hundred and six lamps on one hundred and six iron posts, lit in turn by lamplighters running the circuit, the Kindling taking eleven minutes. The line bows in to a spur at Lamps Thirty-Eight to Forty-One, and Lamp Forty-One, the Far Lamp, stands twenty-two paces from the Well’s rim. The moths that gather at the glass are tallied as favor, and the Line decides who dies lit and who unlit.

When the Legate puts out the Hearth-Lamp under the Interdict the lamps burn on no longer anything at all by the Order’s finding, and the village relights them one by one from its own kitchen fires. The Far Lamp is left dark, so the Ring stands at a hundred and five. Seedlings of the Bloom come up at the foot of every post in the spring, and the Concord of the Two Lamps ends the war.

The manuscript gives the posts, the spacing, the count, the Kindling, the Hearth-Lamp in its niche above the well, and the Far Lamp. The sheet’s closed oval circuit of black iron posts, each about the height of a tall man, its glass lantern heads, its Order’s sun on the Hearth-Lamp, its aerial view round a village with a church and its four state panels Watchful, Kindling, Dusk and Overgrown are its own; the manuscript does not say the Ring is an oval or the posts black, and no lantern is wound in vines. The sheet’s inset captioned The Far Lamp (Lamp Forty-One) shows a plate that reads 106, a slip against its own caption, since Lamp Forty-One is the Far Lamp and 106 is the count of posts. Its title strip, Sacred Doctrine (Doctrine), treats the Ring as a doctrine as the manuscript does.
