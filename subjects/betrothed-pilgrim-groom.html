Betrothed Pilgrim (Groom-to-Be)

Betrothed pilgrim · the cathedral's sixth century · 24 years old

One of a pair of betrothed pilgrims who arrive together in the cathedral's sixth century carrying a folk belief the keepers have heard before and will go on hearing for as long as young couples fall in love within earshot of the legend: that a devotion pure enough, shared rather than solitary, might succeed where every solitary hand has failed. They join their free hands and reach with the others, in a gesture more romantic than any doctrine the Vigil has endorsed, and nothing moves beneath either palm, because wanting to prove a love, however genuine, is still a wanting, and Valeria has never troubled to tell a selfish want from a beautiful one. The manuscript says only “a pair of betrothed pilgrims”; the two are filed as separate subjects, his betrothed beside him.

The sheet gives his age as 24 and draws him rangy, about 5'10", with an open, sunburnt, earnest face, short brown hair, brown eyes, the wisp of a first beard and a bruised thumbnail from smith's work, in a linen shirt, a brown wool tunic and an earth-brown cloak with a pilgrim's cord at the waist and mended boots; he holds her free hand. Its expressions are hopeful, concerned, resolute and quiet joy, and its panels include the pair together on the pilgrimage and on the road to the holy place. Sexes, ages and looks are the sheet's.
