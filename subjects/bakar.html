Bakar

Carter from the plain · regular at the Fourth Cup, Wine Gate of Samaghvto · 47 on his sheet

A stout, damp, philosophical carter from the plain, named Bakar. On the anniversary of the second Portion, in the Fourth Cup, the wine-house at the bottom of the Wine Gate road where carters come to be robbed by highland innkeepers, he says over his fourth cup, with the finality of a man who has been listening to theology all evening: I do not care whose rain it is, so long as it falls on a road I have already paid for. The manuscript calls it the most intelligent remark ever made in the Fourth Cup. Nobody remembers it by morning. The potboy laughs; Medea, pouring the Unclaimed Cup at the empty end of the table, does not.

The manuscript gives his build, his temperament, the remark and the cup, and no age or face. The sheet’s age of 47, stout build of about 5 ft 6 in, round ruddy face with a permanently damp forehead, thinning brown hair under a dark felt cap, heavy dark moustache, hazel eyes, brown wool coat over an off-white shirt, coiled rope at the belt and metal cup are its own, as are its panels Smiling Wryly, Thinking, Serious and Drinking and its scenes In the Wine Gate and A man of the plain, with a cup and a thought. Medea, who pours the Unclaimed Cup, has no sheet in this case and so no page here.

“I do not care whose rain it is, so long as it falls on a road I have already paid for.”
