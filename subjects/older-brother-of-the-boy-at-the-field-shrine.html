The Older Brother of the Boy at the Field Shrine

Older brother of the boy at the field shrine · joins the column, and his brother follows · 19 by his sheet

The older brother of a boy of perhaps fourteen. He joins the column, and the boy joins because he has, wearing an oversized straw rain cape that plainly belongs to his brother, with a short-handled hoe. It is the boy who asks what the column intends to ask the temple for (Part Three, What Three Hundred Became); the older brother is the reason he is there to ask.

The manuscript gives him no age or looks, and the sheet’s are its own. It draws him lean at about 5 ft 7 in, tanned, with a long face and a jutting chin, faint down on the jaw and upper lip, a thin white scar through the left eyebrow, black hair in a short topknot, dark brown eyes, patched hemp clothing, straw sandals and the straw rain cape his brother later borrows, carrying a wooden spade. Its panels are Determined (setting out), Watchful (on the march), Concerned (thinking of his brother) and Steely Resolve (at the shrine); its scenes show him bracing on the spade in the rain and leaving offerings at the field shrine while a smaller figure watches from behind, captioned His younger brother watches him and follows where he goes.
