Pazienza

Mule of the Lamp-Host oil-train · adopted by the Company’s cook · lived to thirty-one · 9 on the sheet

The mule who is called Pazienza has never in her life been a soldier; she was conscripted with Piero, the Lamp-Host oil-porter who dies at the ford of Sant’Elmo in the second winter. Three days later she is found grazing in the willows below the ferry with the Order’s oil-barrel still strapped to her flanks and an expression of the profoundest disapproval. The Company’s cook, who has no use for her, adopts her and feeds her anyway. She lives to be thirty-one, and is buried, by the Company’s own order and at its own cost, under a flat white stone that says only Provision.

The manuscript gives her name, her disapproval, her barrel and her thirty-one years, and no size, colour or markings. The sheet’s age of 9, height of about 1.30 m at the withers, dun brown coat, dark mane, pale muzzle, banded wooden barrel on a pack-saddle and mild long-eared face are its own, as are its panels Calm, Resting, Grazing and Alert and its view of her grazing under a willow. Its age of 9 is its own; the manuscript gives her no age, only the thirty-one years she lives. Its scene Carries the Lamp-Host’s oil through the quiet roads., a rear view at a fence before a hill town, is not in the manuscript. Its title line printing Mule follows the manuscript.
