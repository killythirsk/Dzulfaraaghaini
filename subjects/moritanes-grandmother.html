Moritane's Grandmother

Grandmother of the boy Moritane · lets him plant a paddy unsupervised and never corrects a row · 62 at the planting, by her sheet

The grandmother of Moritane. She lets him, at nine, plant a paddy ankle-deep and unsupervised, and never corrects a row. Watching the plum terrace trampled from his pavilion, he remembers the paddy he planted at nine. The manuscript gives her nothing more than that.

Her age and the sheet are the pack’s. It makes her small and bent at the waist, about 4 ft 10 in (147 cm), with white hair wrapped in a faded cloth, weathered brown skin, a patient half-smile, knotted hands, a faded blue work robe, a straw hat and bare feet in the water, and a worn wooden hoe. Its expression panels are Kind Smile, Amused, Patient and Thoughtful. Its scenes are a full figure leaning on the hoe in a flooded paddy, a back view, Lets Moritane plant a paddy unsupervised, with a boy planting seedlings and the old woman kneeling at his side with her hands in the water, and Watching over Moritane’s work, seen from behind at sunset. The picture has her beside him where the caption and the manuscript say unsupervised.
