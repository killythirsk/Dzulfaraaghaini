Gazetteer of the Yan Empire

About the Map

The empire of Case 4438, drawn as a painted map in a Chinese style, from the Nine-Fold Mountain and the western passes in the north-west, past the capital Yujing and the Jade Hall at the centre, to the wide river-mouth and the flooded ruins on the Azure Sea at the foot. The map is titled Yan Empire; the case is filed under the manuscript's own title, The Heavenly Thunder-Serpent, which is also printed on the map in Chinese characters.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. Beneath the map are a culture key, which tags the map Chinese-inspired / Tang Chinese and leaves natural features and two built places Untagged, and a list of its labels.

Two labels here, Shonavati River and Govinda's Barley Field, echo the map of Amritavana, where Govinda is a grain farmer along the Shonavati. Neither name occurs in the files of Case 4438, and the map does not say that the two cases are connected.

Places

Yan Empire
The empire, printed in the title at the top left. Its emperor is the Yongkang Emperor, nine years into a reign built on nine generations of everyone agreeing not to test whether the Mandate still applied; after the Trial, his son Prince Yue becomes Emperor Yue.
Yujing
Drawn as a walled city at the centre-left, with a seal beside the name. The capital. The Heavenly Thunder-Serpent is brought to it with a new name and a waiting list for its water; Zhou Xuan, the Chief Alchemist, gives it a theology before anyone has thought to measure it, and Mei Suwen, a Court Reckoner with no rank, times it. A three-province coalition under Du Heng reaches its walls and refuses every delegation the dwindling court sends.
Jade Hall
Drawn as a great red-roofed hall south-east of Yujing. In private letters the Ministry of Rites intercepted and wished it had not, Meng Kuo calls the old Emperor the old man in the Jade Hall; Du Heng speaks of the throne as having proved itself unequal to Heaven while the Jade Hall finishes counting its dead.
Jade Terrace
Drawn as a white stepped platform with a pavilion above the Jade Hall. Marked on the map; no case file names it. The files do show the summoned waiting in ranks on a marble terrace beside a great basin for the Second Trial, attendance entirely voluntary; they do not say that this is that terrace.
Nine Bends
Drawn as a river village with boats on a winding river at the left. The fishing village of Ren Duo, who has worked the flooded ruins below it for eleven years, and who tells the district magistrate what he found. The Hermit of Nine Bends, Elder Peng, has lived in the ruins for one hundred and forty-one local years.
Nine-Fold Mountain
Printed across the mountains at the top of the map. The mountain of Master Bai, a cultivator of considerable and largely self-reported fame, who has spent forty years meditating beneath a waterfall in pursuit of the True Yang Breath.
The Western Passes
Printed beside a gate-tower in the mountains at the left. The command of Meng Kuo, nineteen years in command, who arrives at the basin already understanding the true purpose of the summons, and steps in anyway, in full armor, in front of his own soldiers.
The Northern Garrisons
Printed beside walled posts in the north-east. The command of Kang Yi, Grand Marshal, the one senior officer the Second Trial fails to consume, because the prince who wrote its list did not lift the brush when the Marshal's name occurred to him.
The Salt Marshes
Printed in italic over reedy ground in the north-east. The files call Du Heng the Warlord of the Southern Salt Marshes, and put the marshes to the south; the map prints its salt marshes in the north-east. Du Heng stops forwarding the province's tribute to the capital well before the Solstice, begins calling his levies a rescue, and fills the basin with stone.
The Eastern River Garrison
Printed beside two walled posts on the river in the east. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
The Eastern Forests
Printed in italic over pines in the east. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
The Southern Temples
Printed beneath a group of temples south of the Jade Hall. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Govinda's Barley Field
Printed in italic over a field of barley south of Nine Bends. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet. See the note above on Amritavana.
Ferry Crossing
Printed beside a landing stage and ferries where the river reaches the sea. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Shonavati River
Printed in italic along the wide river at the foot of the map. No case file of Case 4438 names it; the Shonavati of the Amritavana map is the river of Case 0188.
The Flooded Ruins
Drawn as stone gateways standing in the shallows at the river mouth. The ruins of an old town that a river drowned, where the creature is found beneath a fallen lintel. Ren Duo has worked them for eleven years, and Elder Peng placed the creature in the basin there four years before the case opens, and left it.
The Sea-Cave
Drawn as a cave mouth in the rocks at the lower left. The district credits the silence of Elder Peng, sun-cured in the ruins under a rotation of identities, to a miracle of the sea-cave that let him survive a wreck.
The Azure Sea
Printed in italic across the water at the foot of the map. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
