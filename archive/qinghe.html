Gazetteer of Qinghe

About the Map

The kingdom of Case 3115, drawn as a river valley between mountains, with the capital Jinlu and the Lantern Ward on the plain to the east, Wanling and Yanshui to the west and Beiyan, the post of the March, at the top left, and the Shuang crossing on the river at the foot. The map is titled Qinghe; the site files the case as The Listening Water, the title of its catalyst.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. The map also draws, without labels, a walled post south-east of the bridge and the river that runs past Jinlu.

Places

Qinghe
The kingdom of Huairen, forty-one years on the throne at seventy-nine, and of his queen Meilan, thirty, later Regent. The map prints no kingdom label; its name is the map's title.
Jinlu
Drawn as a large walled city at the centre-right. The capital: its court is Duan's, a chamberlain of eighty-three who served three kings before Huairen, and Jian's, the Chancellor; its temple is Peizhi's; its palace kitchens are where the Under-Steward buys four crates of ordinary trade stock from the itinerant herbalist (Observer 471), with the Listening Water among them. The peace that ends the war is the Jinlu Concord, signed twenty-six years later.
Lantern Ward
Printed beside a smaller walled quarter east of Jinlu. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Beiyan
Drawn as a walled frontier post with a tower at the top left. The seat of Huaiyu, the King's half-brother, March-Lord of the frontier, who keeps an infant's cap in his campaign chest for the rest of his life and tells no one what it means.
March
Printed in italic over a shaded patch of ground below the northern mountains. The frontier that Huaiyu holds as March-Lord of Beiyan. The files do not otherwise describe it.
Northern Pass
Printed in italic at the top right, beside a gap in the mountains. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Wanling
Drawn as a walled garrison town at the left. The loyalist garrison of General Zhuo, sixty-five, who shelters the two men the Crown wants most, Mingxuan and Duan, when they arrive at his gate needing shelter. It is here that Cao is questioned, with Yancheng's help; Yancheng is killed afterward on a courier's road by young Rao, under Zhuo's own standing order against capture. The Crown's first move against Wanling is carried to it by a herald under a white pennant, who never learns what is in the case.
Yanshui
Drawn as a walled town beside a grid of flooded basins at the lower left. The dukedom of the Duke of Yanshui's Third Son, who inherits it over two cousins with a stronger claim by blood, on the strength of a temple ceremony that says blood was never really the point. Jinlu's court reaches for the case whenever it needs proof that the Father's Reckoning holds even against contradictory evidence.
Shuang
Printed at a bridge over the river at the foot of the map. The files speak of the Shuang ford, not a bridge. At the second autumn's crossing of the ford a seventeen-year-old soldier of the Queen's own garrison (the Soldier Who Killed Huaiyu) sights a mounted man he has been told is a traitor and looses the arrow that kills Huaiyu, the father of the child the garrison exists to protect.
Beiyan Road
Printed in italic along a road at the south-east. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Wanling Road
Printed in italic along a road at the south-west. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
