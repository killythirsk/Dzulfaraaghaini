Gazetteer of the Obliging Sky

About the Map

The country of Case 0233, drawn as thirteen watchtower valleys in the mountains of Vashkeni above the walled city of Samaghvto, the Wine Road running down to it from the north-east, and the plain of Orvaneti and the river Ladzhura below. The map is titled The Obliging Sky, the manuscript's own title; the site files the case as Only a Little, the title on the cover.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. Twelve of the thirteen valleys are named in the files; only Lagoda is not. Ormeli is printed twice, and is listed once, below.

Places

Vashkeni
Printed in italic across the middle of the mountains. The thirteen valleys, each held by a Warden from a tower. By the Concordat of the Quiver the twelve living arrows of the Thirteen-Arrow Bow are shared out among them, four to each of the three gods of the Threefold Crown, and the dead thirteenth is handed in a velvet casket to the Warden of the poorest valley, with the casting voice, which the Hierarchs think a delicious joke.
Zemoli
A valley of the Father, whose tower was reputed to hold the oldest unbroken oath in Vashkeni. Its Warden, Archil, seventy-nine, takes his arrow to the forge at Kharzeni and burns it because the Council that took his oath is dead.
Ghedani
The seat of the family from which the Hierarch Gurgen had come, which makes it the Father's own family tower. Its Warden, Tamaz, declines the forge, holds his arrow for the Father's Hierarch, and carries it into the Fourth Crown on a cushion.
Lagoda
Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Ormeli
Printed twice on the map, once small above the tower and once larger beneath it; the files have one Ormeli. The highest and poorest of the thirteen valleys, held by Ketevan, who enters its water every night in three columns, carried, drunk and lost, and who is given the dead arrow and the thirteenth seat's casting voice. Its steward keeps her letters (the Steward of Ormeli), its hearth-shrine of Elara is Vache's, and two centuries after Ketevan's death it is a lordship whose young archivist opens her ledger at last. It is to Ormeli that the Observer, Besarion the fletcher, comes asking for bread, and leaves the bow.
Tebukhi
A valley of the Son, high and bare and full of sheep. Shalva, its shepherd-lord, puts his arrow on the anvil at Kharzeni without a word.
Tskhavar
One of the Mother's four valleys. Its arrow looses the Long Gentle Rain of the sixth year, the Mother's one Portion in the Concordat's first twenty-eight years; its springs fail by the first of Ferren, and its men march down the Wine Road with the host.
Nakuru
The map prints Nakuru; the case files write Nakuri. A valley of the Mother whose pear orchards stand on high ledges. Its Warden, Revaz, carries the Mother's arrow into the Hall with the face of a bridegroom handing over his sister; it is the arrow the lot picks for the nine days of rain. A pilgrim woman of Nakuri is the first to call Nestan the Little Asker.
Mesvana
One of the Mother's four valleys. Her mill-shrine is kept by an old woman (the Mill-Shrine Keeper), who gives bread at dusk to a small man in a goatherd's coat, Ioseb, and loses the Mother's arrow to him; it is the arrow the king draws to fog the pass at Dzaleti. A vintner and his hound are of its host.
Orsani
A valley of the Father and of quarrymen, whose basalt was split for the Hall of Oaths in which the Council sits. Its Warden, Bidzina, a heavy, honest man, agrees in conscience to lend the Father's arrow to the Sword for the defense of the Hall; a lending is not a loosing, and the arrow becomes the hail.
Kharzeni
The valley of smiths, whose fire has not gone out in four hundred years. In Garren of the thirtieth year its Warden, Levan, Shalva and Archil put three arrows to the anvil-fire in its forge and work the bellows seven hours: the one thing in the valley that was never asked for anything. They are hanged for it on the first of Havren.
Ushkhevi
The valley whose arrow the Son's priests loose in the thirteenth year, when the Son's turn comes round, to hold off the autumn rains until the apricots and the grapes are in: twenty-nine days of clear gold weather, the loveliest vintage anyone can remember, with Otar singing the Dawn Hymn to a table of forty. The Memorandum on Proportion has the other column: Taken from: the plain of Orvaneti.
Dzaleti
A valley of the Son, whose tower stands on a spur at the narrowest place on the only road from the plain into the high valleys. Its Warden, Giorgi, closes the pass to the Sword's army and is answered by a fog: the sentry Gela sits down on the parapet and sleeps through the taking of his own tower. The toll the valley had gathered for four hundred years is then reassigned by a single clerk on a single afternoon.
Bakviri
The gorge so steep that its grapes are picked on ropes, one of the Mother's four valleys. Its Warden, Zurab, has pressed the Mother's wine for forty-four years and pours the cup out empty at the dry Vintage. Otia's mills stand on its river, and Shio, the herald sent up the Wine Road, is of Bakviri.
Wine Road
Drawn as a dashed road from the Tskhavar side of the mountains down to the Wine Gate. On the twenty-fifth of Iverin the Elara host, seven thousand men of Bakviri, Mesvana, Tskhavar and Nakuri with the vintners' ropes still on their shoulders, marches down it with a priest of the Mother carrying the empty cup beside the dry pennant.
Samaghvto
Drawn as a walled city on a rise at the centre. The Place of the Gods: three temples built within a short walk of one another and a Triple Synod that meets four times a year under protest, with the Council's Hall and a garrison of two thousand spearmen, the Sword of Orvaneti, commanded by Guaram. The Observer keeps a stall of plain ash arrows at its Wine Gate for more than thirty years; the Hierarchs of the three gods are Gurgen, Mzia and Otar.
Hall of the Unstrung Bow
Drawn as a fortified hall with a ring in its court. The Hall of Oaths, renamed by Gurgen when the three Hierarchs take it for the Council of the Quiver; the bow lies on a slab in it, tended by the Old Bow-Tender and his apprentice Rati, and the three Wardens who burned their arrows are hanged in it under the Father's justice. Nestan, forty-one, walks eleven days to ask the sky to give less, and dies of the heat in the shade of its north wall.
Temple of Auron
Drawn as a colonnaded temple west of the city. The Father's college, whose Hierarch is Gurgen and whose scribe Sandro numbers the arrows. Marked on the map; no case file describes the building.
Temple of Elara
Drawn as a columned temple among orchards below the city. In its unripe vineyards, on the twenty-eighth of Iverin, three thousand one hundred of the seven thousand vintners of the Elara host, standing in their ranks with ropes on their shoulders, are killed by ice in eleven minutes; there is no first wine for the first time in the temple's history. It is the temple of Mzia, Hierarch of Elara, who bakes the feast bread herself.
Temple of Solan
Drawn as a walled temple with a flame, east of the city. The Son's college, whose Hierarch Otar sings the Dawn Hymn from the high terrace of the temple to a sun that had not been told which side to take, while the valleys of the Son sit out the march in weighted neutrality.
Wine Gate
Drawn as the gatehouse at the south-east corner of the city, with the Ladzhura flowing out beside it. The Observer's stall stands here, with its plain ash arrows at the same price to everyone; Ioseb lives above a glovemaker's shop behind it, and three cases of the Epilogue hold only plain ash cut at a stall at the Wine Gate.
The Fourth Cup
Drawn as a long wine-house among vineyards below the Wine Gate. The wine-house at the bottom of the Wine Gate road, where carters come to be robbed by highland innkeepers. Medea is its proprietor's daughter and later its proprietress, who pours the Unclaimed Cup; Bakar the carter says the most intelligent thing ever said in it, and is forgotten by morning; the potboy is the one who laughed; and the Observer nurses one cup of watered wine in its corner.
Dzirvali
Drawn as a walled palace town of mud-brick in the east. The seat of Orvan, king of Orvaneti, and of Tinatin, who keeps a small wooden spoon in her sleeve; their son Mirdat is crowned there, with a mastiff, Lomi, beneath the throne, and the royal kitchens are the Palace Cook's.
Ladzhura
Drawn as a river running south from the Wine Gate past Dzirvali into marshland. The six hundred thousand souls along it grow half of what Vashkeni eats. In the nine days of rain over the plain it rises and takes four hundred and six. Mirdat pictures a plot of barley on its east bank, with a boundary ditch and a single thorn tree, where his mother knelt in the first spring of his life to show him how to sow.
Orvaneti
Printed across the plain at the foot of the map. The kingdom of the plain, which has neither a seat on the Council of the Quiver nor an arrow, and which pays its tribute twice after the Synod's verdict on a famine nobody caused.
Empire of the Four Crowns
Printed in spaced italic letters across the foot of the map. The empire whose emperor leaves no heir and no will, only a ledger in his own hand listing the arrows and the dead; it breaks into thirteen lordships, and the ten spent shafts are there for the buying.
