Gazetteer of Danjeri

About the Map

The kingdom of Case 0113, drawn from the northern wells and the river mines at the top of the map down the river to a thatched village at its foot, with Kambaso beside the river, the eastern road running out to the Ghelo salt-pans on the rim of the white desert, and Bantara beyond them. The map is titled for the kingdom alone; the manuscript's own title is The War the Dog Never Fought, and the site files the case as The Dog Is Not in It.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. The map also draws, without labels, a second gatehouse at its centre, a second crossing of the river below the ford, the village at its foot and the river itself.

Places

Danjeri
The kingdom, printed in large letters across the middle of the map. Its kings have been Weighed onto the throne for four hundred years by a rite built never to give a straight answer, and the war of this case begins when it is asked to give one.
The Northern Wells
Printed in the north beside three drawn wells. When Fode asks whether a man who loved an animal might do best to carry it beyond the northern wells, Kolo does not look up from the fish he is sorting: I was told never.
The River Mines
Printed in the north-west beside three dark pits on the river. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Kambaso
Drawn as a walled fortress town beside the river, the largest place on the map. The palace is here, with the Ancestor Court beside it where each king stands to be Weighed. The lower town has its beer-houses, among them Hawa's Weighmaster's Table at the lower end of the gold-weighing yard, and its lower granary, where Bocar goes on knotting his cords through the winter of the war long after there is anything in the bin to measure. It is where Sana carries the Cell into the palace, and where Queen Awa leaves, on the second night of the war, by the eastern gate.
The Eastern Road
Drawn as a dashed road running east from Kambaso to Ghelo, and on across the desert to Bantara. When Awa leaves Kambaso she has the toll-houses of the eastern road already in her pocket; after the Bantaran riders come, neither claimant can afford to defend them, and neither does.
Ghelo
Drawn as a gatehouse beside a grid of salt basins. These are the Ghelo salt-pans: shallow evaporation basins walled with clay and raked by hand by two thousand pan-workers, from which nine of every ten measures of salt in the kingdom came, and from whose tolls the crown had drawn a third of its revenue for four hundred years. It is on the road between Kambaso and the pans that Sana takes in the half-grown stray he names Doni. In Deyren of the second spring of the war the Bantaran riders take them.
The White Desert
Printed in spaced italic letters across the east. The Ghelo pans lie on its rim.
Bantara
Drawn as a cluster of flat-roofed houses on grey ground in the far east. A kingdom of caravan-towns and horse-lords two weeks' march beyond the Ghelo salt-pans, which had grown fat on the same trade roads Danjeri guarded. In the second spring of the war its horse-lords ride in with four thousand riders, take the pans and the ford of the Second Road while they are about it, and do not ride out again.
The Ford of the Second Road
Drawn as a crossing of the river at the left of the map, with the Second Road running east from it. The Bantaran riders take the ford along with the pans, and neither is recovered for a generation. Sekou, who reigns twenty-six years more, rebuilds it in the eleventh year.
The Second Road
Drawn as a dashed road running east from the ford. The case files name it only for the ford, and in the title of one scene on the Bantaran Horse-Lords' sheet, The Ghelo Salt Pans — The Second Road.
The River-Road
Drawn as a dashed road running south along the river to the village at the foot of the map. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
