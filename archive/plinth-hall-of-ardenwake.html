Gazetteer of the Plinth Hall of Ardenwake

About the Sheet

The hall of Ardenwake's cathedral in which Valeria rests on an open plinth, drawn as a character-style sheet rather than a map and titled Plinth Hall of Ardenwake: The Second Most Important Element. The manuscript of Case 0000B gives only a plinth, a hall, a spire, steps, a north face, a southern transept and a law over the outer gate; almost everything else the sheet draws is its own.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the sheet's panels name; each note is drawn from the manuscript, and where the manuscript says nothing the note says so.

Places

The Plinth
The waist-high stone plinth at the centre of the nave, on which Valeria rests open to any hand. The sheet gives it as about 3 ft high and 4 ft across, in plain grey stone with a polished top. The manuscript bears out the plainness: the Thief runs his fingers along its base in the dark, looking for a hidden catch, lodestone or counterweight, and finds the same stone as the rest of the hall.
The Nave
The long stone hall in which every attempt is made, drawn from the pilgrims' end with the plinth under a tall window. The sheet calls it a long stone nave-hall of battle-worn grey stone. It is where a soldier of the ninth siege walks in from the breach, still bleeding, and where a grieving mother weeps for the better part of an hour.
The Spire
The sheet gives it as about 180 ft. The manuscript has it visible from every quarter of the city, which is why the Corvane engineers of the eleventh siege train their siege engines first on the spire rather than the outer wall: Berant argues that watching your own god's house take damage in plain daylight does more to a garrison's nerve than watching an ordinary gatehouse fall.
The Gate Inscription
The law carved above the cathedral's outer gate, drawn on the sheet as lettered stone: any soul may attempt the blade if they arrive on foot, without banner, without escort, without an armed man at their back. The manuscript says the law never mentions Ardwen, faith or humility by name; belief was never required, arrival was. None of the eleven armies ever came that way. See the Vigil of Ardwen.
The Pilgrim Steps
The worn stone stair up from the square to the hall's doors. The manuscript's children take turns walking the last few steps down with the slack look of a failed Attempter, the Nothing Face.
The Pilgrim's Entrance
The sheet's view down the empty nave from the doors toward the plinth: the way every Attempter comes in, on foot and unarmed. The manuscript describes no entrance beyond the law over the gate.
The Empty Hand
The sheet's panel of that name draws a crowd of hooded pilgrims seen from behind, facing the nave. The Empty Hand is Ardenwake's own name for the attempt, win or lose, so often spoken in the streets around the hall that visiting pilgrims mistook it for the sword's second title.
The Final Appeal
The sheet's panel of that name draws a hooded figure kneeling at the plinth, hands reaching for the sword. The sheet does not say who it is. The manuscript has many kneelers, among them Adouma, who kneels as his mother taught him to kneel before rain, and a grieving mother.
The Anchorite's Cell
Drawn on the sheet as a plain stone wall with a narrow slot. In the manuscript the cell is built against the cathedral's north face, sealed past the width of a single door and fed through a slot too narrow to pass anything larger than bread and water; the Anchorite lives in it for eleven years before the siege. The keepers who kneel at it include a newly assigned keeper and the keeper who pressed gently.
The North Face
The sheet draws it twice, after the siege, as a ruin of fallen stone open to the daylight. In the eleventh siege Corvane's sappers bring the whole face down at midday in a single controlled collapse, cell and all, and the breach lets daylight into the hall for the first time in the Anchorite's eleven years of enclosure.
After the Eleventh Siege
The sheet's closing panel: the hall with its wall in rubble and daylight pouring through. In the manuscript the Anchorite sets Valeria back across the plinth herself and refuses to be named; her old cell does not survive.
The Southern Transept
Not drawn on the sheet. The manuscript names it once: Xu Lian is cataloguing grain counts in a side chamber off the southern transept when the shudder of the falling wall reaches him.
