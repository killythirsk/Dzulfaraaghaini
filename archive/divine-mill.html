Gazetteer of the Divine Mill

About the Map

The country of Case 2140, drawn as the river Sevra running down the middle of the map, with Ostrevenna and Trevano on its west bank, the marshy barony of Verrasco on its east bank, the farms of Vardane to the south-east, and a single mill with a water wheel and a bell tower beside a jetty at the foot. The map is titled The Divine Mill, the manuscript's own title; the site files the case as The Third Grain, the title on the cover.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. The map also draws, without labels, a small church by the bend of the river, a farmhouse east of Verrasco, farms and chapels around Vardane, and the one mill. The files' county mill is the mill of Vardane; the map draws only one mill and does not name it.

Places

Sevra
Printed in italic beside the river that runs down the middle of the map. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Ostrevenna
Drawn as a walled hill fortress at the top left. The seat of the family of Garibald, Count of Vardane, who breaks down completely here, the first time anyone has ever seen him do so, when Baron Landulf files his claim and he offers no defense. It is the table of Adelrada, Elder of House Ratchis, seventy-eight, who sends the ruling to her notary Vitalis rather than accept a summary of it, and who keeps for two years a private accounting that her great-nephew Ranulf finds after her death.
Elbow
Printed beside the bend in the river. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Trevano
Drawn as a walled town of towers and tiled roofs at the left. The duchy of Grimoald, who is forty-six when a badly garbled rumor of a Verrasco mill reaches him and who sends his engineer Gisulf to look, report honestly and touch nothing. Gisulf's eleven-page report reaches one conclusion: a tolerance that nothing in Trevano's armories can presently match. Two years of failing to reproduce the bearing leave Trevano with lathes, measurement and metalworkers trained to a precision no duchy has demanded, and Grimoald gives the order to turn them toward siege engines.
Verrasco
Printed in italic on a marsh of reeds east of the river, bounded by a drawn line. The barony of Landulf, who inherits it at nineteen when his older brother Ansfrid falls from a lathered horse on a boar hunt, and whose daughter Gisela has read her father's ledgers long enough to know how far short of a good marriage its revenues fall. He watches Vardane's fortunes climb for three years, files a formal claim under the Writ of Undeclared Grace, and wins, and holds the mill until House Ratchis finds an unconnected claimant to take it back. He dies without ever suspecting it.
Vardane
Printed beside a farmstead of barns and chapels in the south-east. The county of Count Garibald, who shelters a traveling clockmaker (Observer 419) for a night's lodging and sets one condition on House Ratchis reclaiming the mill for him: that it cost Landulf nothing beyond what the war his own claim set in motion has already cost him. The mill goes back to Vardane exactly as quietly as it left. Its county mill is kept by Teudis, the broad, uncomplaining miller, whose own tavern's telling of the visit gives the county its name for what changed the wheel: the Third Grain. Garibald returns to it five years later to a wheel still turning and a miller two winters dead.
