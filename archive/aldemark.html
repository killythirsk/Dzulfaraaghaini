Gazetteer of Aldemark

About the Map

The kingdom of Case 1268, drawn as the capital Dornhal at the top centre and the ten villages around it that the probes overhead chose, one at a time, eleven days apiece and eleven months apart. The map is titled Aldemark; the site files the case as Independent Observation, the title on the cover, and the manuscript's own title is The Eleventh Astral Dream.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. The files name the ten villages as Halstead, Ansdorf, Grenmoor, Wulfsholt, Faldrun, Brandwick, Harkendale, Stonewick, Aschendale and Reinfeld, and they are listed here in that order, which is not the order of the map. The map also draws, without labels, two chapels, a village beyond the western border and the river at the top.

Places

Aldemark
The kingdom, printed in large letters at the left-centre. The land under the Threefold Crown, whose high air holds the Probe Network, dormant for generations before the first dream, which on a schedule fixed before the seeding lends one village at a time a seat in the dark above its own world: no voice, no doctrine and no instruction, only altitude.
Dornhal
Drawn as a walled city with a church tower at the top centre. The seat of the Crown: its Wardens and its Inquisitors, and the Triarchs Ansovald, Adaltrude and Sigward, who order the purges. Hraban is recalled to it the spring before the eleventh selection, and the eleventh falls on the whole capital, wall to wall, on every Warden, priest and Triarch asleep inside it; the guard it rotates to keep itself awake holds for six days, and on the seventh a sentry dreams the whole of it in a single breath. The Anchorite of Ardwen answers every question an Inquisitor puts to her here and tells him nothing.
Halstead
Drawn as a village with a church tower in the middle of the map. The first selection. On the first night of Brayen, in the iron cold of early spring that Aldemark's peasants call Elara's Fast, Ymma wakes her husband before dawn to tell him she has been nowhere and everywhere; by the second week no one in Halstead is calling it hunger. Otker, a farmhand saving barley-money for a daughter's loom, goes into the fire on the last day of Brayen without recanting. The Warden Hraban is sent to it from Ansdorf on the twentieth day of the dreaming, and a woman in undyed grey wool kneels in its ashes for the better part of an afternoon and asks the sky to let her come.
Ansdorf
Drawn as a village with a church tower south of Dornhal. The district seat, from which Hraban is sent to Halstead and to which he writes for guidance before punishment, and from which the answer comes sealed with the joined sigil of all three Triarchs. The Anchorite is already here on the ninth day of its dreaming, where a junior Warden interviews her and files the result unread. Ansovald purges it with far less debate than Halstead, having concluded that debate is itself a contagion.
Grenmoor
Drawn as a village in the north-west. The village that sings on its way to the burning. The dreaming is taken here as a blessing sent down by Solan, the god of fire and rebellion and the wide bright sky, and the villagers raise a shrine of fieldstone and unbleached cloth to what they call the Star-Walkers, before which the Anchorite tells no one which god she thinks the singing was for.
Wulfsholt
Drawn as a village in the south-west. One of the ten villages the network chose. No case file describes it further.
Faldrun
Drawn as a village in the north-east. The Anchorite blesses a blacksmith's iron globe here in Ardwen's name, while the village watches. The village is one of the ten the network chose.
Brandwick
Drawn as a village in the south-east. One of the ten villages the network chose. No case file describes it further.
Harkendale
Drawn as a village on the edge of the eastern hills. The Anchorite is here in its root cellars, warned more than once to move on.
Stonewick
Drawn as a village on the lower slopes of the eastern hills. One of the ten villages the network chose. No case file describes it further.
Aschendale
Drawn as a village at the foot of the map. The one village that is never purged. At its boundary stone, where no purge is ever ordered, the Anchorite prays that night for the elders who chose a hard truth over an easy lie.
Reinfeld
Drawn as a village at the top of the map, on the road north of Dornhal. One of the ten villages the network chose. The Anchorite is taken here on charges of vagrancy and obstruction, and asks the soldier who binds her wrists whether he was at Halstead. Sigward signs the order for its purge himself, rather than delegate it, having made it a point of pride never to ask a Warden to carry out a purge he is not willing to have his own name on.
Eastern Hills
Printed in italic along the mountains on the right. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
Western Marches
Printed in italic on shaded ground at the left. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet. The Anchorite of Ardwen is an anchorite of the western border.
River Districts
Printed in italic on shaded ground at the foot of the left-hand side. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
