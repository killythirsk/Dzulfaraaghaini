Gazetteer of the Unclouded

About the Map

The valley of Case 3312, drawn as rice terraces and low hills between two rivers, with the walled enclosure of Takashiro under its keep to the west, a plum terrace and a pond with a pavilion at the centre, and the temple of Jakuen-ji on the eastern edge. The map is titled The Unclouded, the manuscript's own title; the site files the case as The Debt Is Settled, the title on the cover.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case’s thirty-four subjects, the farmers and children who carry the story, Isamu, Genzo, Chiyo, Kiyo and Sachi among them, and the court diviner Tadaie, now with their sheets. The map also draws, without labels, a castle keep on a stone base above Takashiro, a pond with a pavilion, a village at the crossing of the road, a torii gate and several farmsteads. The files speak of an open pavilion from which Moritane watches his garden, of a field shrine where notched sticks are laid on the lowest step, and of a market town where Sachi reads a debt aloud from a cart; they do not say whether these are the pavilion, the torii gate and the village that the map draws. They also speak of the court of Himuro, a realm of terraced river valleys, which the map does not label and the files do not place against Kagetani.

Places

Takashiro
Printed beneath a walled enclosure of tiled houses beside the western road, with a castle keep on a stone base standing above it. The files name it once, as the garrison whose drummer, a boy of sixteen, strikes the evening drum at the gatehouse as the column arrives, and goes on striking it after the drum has stopped meaning anything, until Norimasa, who has ordered the garrison to hold the gate rather than the road, lays a hand over the drumstick. They do not say that Takashiro is the lord's seat, or that its keep is the castle beside which Moritane's garden grows.
Kagetani
Printed in italic across the lower valley. The files use the name for the house, the domain and the garden. Moritane is its seventh lord; Sadatomo manages its household office, and Norimasa, who drafts its tax increases and negotiates its loan with Jakuen-ji when the garden's bills outrun the domain, is left holding its actual administration when the garden is gone. A debt of eleven koku recurs door to door among its tenants, as Isamu finds, among them the eldest son of the farmer Genzo, and Sachi's Brother, taken two years before the march against the same figure owed to a different temple. The garden is the one the chronicles name the uprising after. When the domain ends, an overlord three provinces off, to whom Kagetani has been a name he has had no cause to remember in six generations, sends a letter of condolence to the wrong address and then a magistrate to the right one, who confirms Norimasa in the administration he already holds and asks what became of the garden.
Plum Terrace
Printed in italic beside a round orchard of plum blossom, set in rings of terraces, north of the pond. The newest terrace of Moritane's garden, laid out by the Head Gardener of Kagetani, entirely by eye and in a single feverish week, as a spiral winding outward from the water whose rings hold one tree, then one, two, three, five, eight and thirteen. Its trees bloom out of season under paper screens and charcoal braziers, and the gardener keeps a knotted tally-cord of the garden's blossoms for reasons he never gives. It is where the garden ends: Moritane watches the terrace trampled from his pavilion and dies in its ruin, and Norimasa is the one who finds him, and is sick against the ruined terrace. The files say the spiral winds outward from the water; the map draws a pond and a pavilion beside the orchard and labels neither.
Jakuen-ji
Printed beside a temple with a pagoda at the right edge of the map. The temple whose lending the valley owes. Ennen, a monk of fifty-three by his sheet, administers it the way a physician gives bitter medicine, and has made four exceptions in eleven years, until the gift bundle that Sadatomo arranges carries the stone to him; the exceptions stop there. He receives the loan that Norimasa negotiates for the eastern tenants, and presides over the clause that takes Chiyo and Kiyo, and Genzo’s eldest son, into service as collateral. A novice receives Isamu there at night, can answer nothing he asks, and goes to fetch the steward who can; that steward, tall and thin, announces that four further koku have accrued as a maintenance charge and the term is extended by four months. A second steward, the keeper of the ledger room as the pack draws him, is the one who tells Isamu that the debt is settled, the sentence of the case's cover title. Ennen meets the crowd at the ruin of the room where the loan was signed, and the stone is later found on his body by Isamu. The files do not say where that room stood, or whose gates are brought down when a man from a neighboring village finds Chiyo and Kiyo alive among some thirty held persons.
