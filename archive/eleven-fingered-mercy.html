Gazetteer of the Eleven-Fingered Mercy

About the Map

The kingdom of Case 1153, drawn as a ring of mountains with Tavgora on its rock at the centre, a road running west along the western range to Otsane and east to the eastern passes, and the northern passes behind the terraces. The map is titled The Eleven-Fingered Mercy, the manuscript's own title; the site files the case as The Second Hand, the title on the cover.

It is filed here as a gazetteer, not as a subject or a record. The places below are the ones the map labels; each note is drawn from the case files, and a place that no case file yet describes is said to be unfiled rather than guessed at. The map also draws, without labels, a watchtower on the northern road, a village below the eastern passes, and a small building where the river forks south of the city. The files speak of a small shrine in a lower valley that keeps a gilded wooden hand with too many fingers; they do not say whether it is this building.

Places

Sarkveti
The kingdom, printed on a shaded patch east of Tavgora. A kingdom of terraces that believes anything worth having must be climbed for, and whose temple keeps the rolls on which a prince's name becomes a fact. The war that follows the Proving takes a winter and part of a spring and a little under two thousand lives, and leaves a kingdom that owns a doctrine that can be used, and is used by it, reign after reign, until Corvane absorbs what is left.
Tavgora
Drawn as a walled hill city on a crag at the centre of the map. The capital: its palace has a physicians' wing where Levan would rather be than in the training yard, its lower market has the stall of the clockmaker (Observer 419), and the temple of Auron here is Demna's. Guram walks its northern wall with Vazha and puts both hands over his face; Bagrat learned to swim in the cold river below its walls, and was carried to its gates fevered past recognizing his own name, as Sulqan, the border garrison's healer, watched. In the war its outer wall goes to Bagrat's siege engines after Vazha's faction has refused three offers of surrender, and Gia holds the eastern breach with the household guard; Otar and eleven of Levan's former patients lead the surgeon out by a goat track on the night the wall falls.
Otsane
Drawn as a cluster of houses at the end of the western road. A small theocracy tucked into a fold of the western range so thoroughly unremarkable that, in the files, Sarkveti's own maps render it as an unlabelled patch of grey; this map names it. Amets, Keeper of the Vigil of Ardwen there, takes in the stranger Otar's men have delivered to her gate and, in the one lie of her life, tells the one rider who does reach Otsane that no man of that name ever came. Levan lives there thirty-one more years as Mikel, in a small, mostly unpaid surgery, and dies at sixty-three. Bagrat spends nearly six of his twenty-six years as king searching every unmapped fold of the western range for him.
The Western Range
Printed in italic along the road between Tavgora and Otsane. The range in whose folds Otsane is tucked, and the one Bagrat searches.
Northern Passes
Printed in the north above a watchtower. Marked on the map; no case file names a pass. The files do speak of a northern answer to the Vashtir raids, which Bagrat leads in a night snowstorm, and from which he is carried home over eight days with his sword arm swollen black to the elbow.
The Eastern Passes
Printed in the east beside a village. Marked on the map; no case file describes it yet.
