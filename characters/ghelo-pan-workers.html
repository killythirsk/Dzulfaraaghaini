Ghelo Pan-Workers

Two thousand rakers of the Ghelo salt-pans · where nine of every ten measures of the kingdom's salt came from · 16 to 60 on their sheet

The Ghelo salt-pans lie two weeks east of Kambaso on the rim of the white desert: a shimmering plain of shallow evaporation basins, walled with clay and raked by hand by two thousand pan-workers, from which nine of every ten measures of salt in the kingdom came, and from whose tolls the crown had drawn a third of its revenue for four hundred years. In Deyren of the second spring of the war the Bantaran riders take them. The manuscript says nothing of what happens to the workers: whether they flee, stay, are paid, or work under new masters. Around them the accounts shift: the salt caravans are diverted through Bantaran towns, the sorghum stands unharvested in the flood-plain, and a measure of millet in Kambaso sells for what a measure of gold-dust cost in Tiemoko's day.

The manuscript gives them their number and their rakes; it gives no names, ages, faces or later appearance. The sheet's ages of 16 to 60, lean sinewy stooped builds, deep brown salt-crusted skin, cropped or wrapped hair, dark-brown eyes, rake-worn hands, straw hats and clay-grey basins are its own, as is its remark that they work under Bantaran riders after the pans fall. Its panels are Endurance, Exhaustion, Quiet Defiance and Watchful Fear, and its scenes At the Pans, Raking the Basins, Salt-Dried Faces and Under Bantaran Riders. The riders who take the pans have a page of their own.

“raked by hand by two thousand pan-workers”
