← Case 0277 · The Uncounted Variable

Gertrud

Miller's widow of Muhlbach · lost two sons in a border skirmish history did not name · mid-fifties by her sheet

A miller's widow in Muhlbach, a village of perhaps forty roofs around a mill wheel that has not stopped turning, by any villager's memory, in eleven decades. She lost two sons to a border skirmish history did not think important enough to name, and decided some years ago to feed anyone who arrives at her door looking as she imagines her sons might have looked, had either of them lived long enough to look tired. She takes in the girl, the guard and the hermit without a single question. It is, Hildegund will reflect many years later, the first ordinary evening of her entire life, no herald announcing the courses, no taster, no third of the table reserved for men whose only function was to watch the other two-thirds, and she does not know what to do with her hands until Vara sets a knife and a bowl of unshelled peas in front of her.

Two days behind them a larger, less careless patrol of Otwin's men questions her with a courtesy that unsettles her rather more than threats would. It gets the direction it wants, thanks her, pays for the bread it ate and rides on without overturning a chair, because a village that notices it has been robbed talks, and one visited by polite strangers does not. In the alehouses of Muhlbach, where they had actually fed him, the preferred theory of the hermit is that he was nobody at all. The manuscript gives her no appearance; the sheet's mid-fifties, grey hair under a plain wool headscarf, sturdy thick-armed build and apron, and flour worked into the creases of her hands are its own.
