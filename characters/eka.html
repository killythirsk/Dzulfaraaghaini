Eka

Bonesetter's daughter · physician of Tavgora · early-to-mid 30s on the sheet

A bonesetter's daughter who spends a month watching Levan's surgery from the doorway before asking whether she might hold the instruments, and is told she may, provided she puts them back in order. She trains with him for the better part of a decade and becomes the nearest thing Tavgora has to a second such hand: one of the two physicians at Bagrat's six-hour surgery, who stop trying to follow what Levan is doing in the fourth hour and simply hand him whatever he silently reaches for. During the siege she keeps her own surgery open and asks exactly one question of every man carried in. The hardest hour of the war, she will say decades later, is the morning she treats two men from the same street who put those wounds on each other, side by side, each asking whether the other will live. Levan hands her the one-eyed grey cat before he leaves. Her craft survives one generation of apprentices, and not a second.

The text gives her no physical description beyond being a young woman and a skilled physician; her sheet says everything else on it, a sturdy, dark-haired, dark-eyed figure in a bloodstained apron with chapped, over-washed hands, is invented for consistency.
