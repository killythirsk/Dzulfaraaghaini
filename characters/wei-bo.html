Wei Bo

Calligraphy instructor of the Hollow Pine Sect · 52 by his sheet

A soft-spoken calligraphy master who has never in his life held a blade in anger and long ago stopped pretending he ever would, and the sect's only other instructor of any seniority. He stays behind, as he always does, to keep the mountain's books and its brushes in order while the rest chase duels and errands, and alone among them has never once complained of the arrangement. When Kuang Zhi climbs the mountain to die, Wei Bo is the one left at the school.

Nine students are with him when the six hundred arrive: three surrender and are spared for information they do not have, two flee, and four gather at the courtyard gate before dawn behind him. In the last quiet hour he grinds fresh ink for a lesson no student will ever receive, because his hands need something familiar to hold and calligraphy is the only thing they have ever been steady enough for. When the forward line comes round the lower switchback he sets the ink aside, takes up a training staff meant for demonstration, and tells the other three that a school worth teaching in is worth dying in front of, the only complete sentence of his that anyone troubles to remember, which the histories will misremember as heroism and not the plain grief it was. Shen Lie learns of it from Bo, and rebuilds the sect on Wei Bo's memory. His sheet draws him slight and pale from indoor work, with ink-stained fingers, a thin trimmed mustache and a topknot; its age and build are its own.
