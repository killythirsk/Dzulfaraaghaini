The Keeper's mule · carries Alazaïs from Sant-Sever · 12 years old by her sheet

The mule on which Alazaïs rides the two days from the House of Vigil at Sant-Sever to the temple at Castelmaur, and on which she leaves the mill gate in the cold light of the second of Iverin. Her name means patience, and the manuscript is careful to record that she possesses none: she has not been consulted about the Keeper's midnight walk to the mill, and grumbles in the rear of it. The lay-brother Joan, who carries the Keeper's saddlebag, is afraid of her through the whole of the digging, until the burying of the swarm leaves him afraid of nothing.

The manuscript gives her no age, colour or build. The sheet's twelve years, stocky four-foot-ten, dusty grey-brown coat, notched left ear, drooping lips, patched wool saddle-cloth and dark, unimpressed eyes are its own, and its captions, Suspicion, Alarmed by Joan, Stubborn Resolve, Impatient Grumble, are the nearest the file comes to her opinion of any of it.
