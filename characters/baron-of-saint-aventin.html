Baron of Saint-Aventin

Eldest of the Threefold barons · unnamed in the manuscript · 62 years old by his sheet

He is a letter. The war, says the manuscript, took the better part of two winters to discover that it was one, and the discoveries came in the order in which a ledger reveals a loss: first a letter, then a date, then a wall. The letter comes on the ninth of Iverin under the colors of the Baron of Saint-Aventin, eldest of the Threefold barons, who writes to the Hierarch at Castelmaur in a hand that does not trouble with courtesy that eleven barons of the south will pay a tenth of their grain to no creditor in grey, and asks how many men the temple can put on a road by the spring. He writes four days after the Hierarch has declared the Accord of Montclar void on the fifth of Iverin, and what he asks for is soldiers. The date is the Last Order's, calling the crown's loan on the first of Kerren; the wall is at Montclar.

That is the whole of what the manuscript gives him: a title, a seniority, a colour, a number of fellow barons and a tone of voice. He has no name, no face, no age, and no reply is recorded, though the Hierarch has been believed by eleven of the realm's seventeen barons, who are Threefold. The sheet's sixty-two years, stout five-foot-eight, florid jowled face, sharp dark-brown eyes, silver hair worn long to the collar, forked silver beard, gold ring on every finger of the right hand, scarlet-and-gold quilted surcoat, fur mantle and jeweled sword are its own, and so is the line it prints beside him, No creditor in grey shall take our grain, which is the sheet's gloss on the letter and not a quotation from it.
