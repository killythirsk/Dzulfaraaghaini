The Doctrine of the Unbroken Hand

Auron temple archive · codified in the same century as the Proving, four centuries before the events

An older and considerably colder text than the Proving that shares its theology, filed in the temple archive alongside two dozen other doctrines no living Hierarch had needed to cite: that a king's hand is the instrument of Auron's law made visible, and that no man missing the wholeness Auron gave him at birth can be trusted to hold that law without some essential piece of it also missing. It speaks of men who lack the god's gift and says nothing of men who have been given another, and it is in that silence that Vazha's allies and Bagrat's lawyers both make their camps. The form that turns it into procedure, the Writ of Examination, sits beside it in the same archive, and the paragraph beneath the summons fixes what a court of three is to do on a finding of counterfeit.

Once it has been shown to be usable, it is used: against a nephew with a limp in the second reign, a daughter with a squint in the third, a prince with a withered foot in the fourth, until succession in Sarkveti is a contest of defect rather than claim, and the temple that charges a fee for each examination grows rich as the crown grows poor. The manuscript never says what it looks like; the sheet draws it as a bound codex of yellowed vellum with an iron-grey seal and a wood-and-iron clasp.
