Bocar's Daughter

Daughter of the lower granary's keeper · unnamed · 9 years old

Nine years old on the night her father, Bocar, ties the last knot on the cord for his own household: the night his daughter stopped asking whether the line was moving. Her father sits down on the bench beside the empty bin the next day and does not get up. She keeps his cords. His daughter would keep the cords for forty-one years, and would be found, when she died, to have added a knot to each of them every winter, for reasons nobody who knew her could have given, and which she could not have given either. The manuscript does not name her, say who raised her, or give her age at death, and does not mention her again.

The manuscript gives her age, her question and her forty-one years. The sheet's small thin build of about 4 ft 3 in (130 cm), deep brown skin, solemn round face, large dark-brown eyes, two short plaits tied with twine, missing front tooth and faded earth-brown and ochre wrapper are its own, as are its panels Patience (Waiting), Grief (Silent), Listening (No Questions) and Resolve (Each Winter). Its captions, The Cords, Kept for 41 years and She stops asking if the line is moving; each winter, she adds a knot to her father's cords., follow the manuscript.
