← Case 0113 · The Dog Is Not in It

Dembele

Fishmonger to the palace kennel for thirty years · the one dissenter · 55 years old on his sheet

One man in the Weighmaster's Table dissents from the theories about the palace dog. He was the fishmonger Dembele, who had sold the palace kennel its dried fish for thirty years, and who had concluded, on the evidence of thirty years of invoices, that the animal was merely a dog with a very good accountant. He says so once, on a slow evening, to the room at large: Nothing that eats fish, he says, has ever been a god. The manuscript's verdict is flat and complete: He was, by the standards of every age before and since, entirely right, and the beer-house forgot him within the week.

He has no other scene. The fish he sold is the dried fish of the treasury's ration line, dried fish, six, though the manuscript does not say whether the invoices he held were for that line. It does not say what became of him, or whether he lived to see the dog killed. Hawa's beer-house, where he spoke, forgets him; Kolo later buys the dog's fish with his own copper in the lower market, and the manuscript does not connect the two.

The manuscript gives his trade, his thirty years and his sentence. The sheet's age of 55, wiry build, deep brown skin, shaved head, dark brown eyes, short grey goatee, fish-scale flecks on the forearms and tally cords, undyed cloth and worn leather apron are its own, as are its panels Skepticism, Dry Amusement, Defiance and Quiet Resolve, its title strip, Palace Kennel Fishmonger, and its scene At the Weighmaster's Table, which prints his sentence unbroken: Nothing that eats fish has ever been a god.

“the animal was merely a dog with a very good accountant”
