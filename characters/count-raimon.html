Count Raimon of Rocafort

Count of Rocafort · neighbor and rival of Castelmaur · old and lean; about 64 years old by his sheet

An old, lean count who rules Rocafort, a smaller, poorer, more patient neighbor of Castelmaur, from beyond the faint blue line of hills, and who collects prisoners as other men collect falcons. For forty years he and the baron have each claimed a small weir on the Sarrac; in the late summer his riders are already waiting at the ford when Guilhem arrives with forty men, and take the weir, the men, two banners and the baron's brother in a morning. The ransom he names, in a courteous letter, is four hundred solidi, in coin, to be counted at Rocafort in the presence of his chaplain. He allows Guilhem one letter a fortnight, under seal, to be read first by the chaplain.

When Guilhem dies of the cough he said he had, on the fourteenth of Lorren, the count, being a man of honor, returns the body without ransom, and keeps the weir. Castelmaur loses two more villages to his writ in the autumn; in the fourth year he acquires the ridge district of Lasserra, finding that it pays more in peace than it would in coin. Folquet becomes clerk of record to his assizes, and a sleek, well-read judge of his court reads a Saracen into the tenth line of an old ledger. The sheet notes that the source gives only “old”, “lean” and the falcon comparison, and that the rest is invented; its sixty-four years, white hair and pale grey eyes are not the manuscript's.
