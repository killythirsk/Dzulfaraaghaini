Sexton

Sexton of the cathedral bell-loft · reads the water-clock · 57 years old by his sheet

The man who tolls the hours from the cathedral bell-loft. On the night the Uncounted Day begins he reads his water-clock by a candle and tolls midnight: a single deep bronze note and then eleven others, each one slower than the last, like a man descending a stair in the dark. It carries across the whole of the Low Market, over the dark roofs of the city, and it is what moves the clerk Wido, standing in the second rank of the Grey Lances before the Low Forge, to say that it is past the hour. A Hierarch's sexton keeps the ox Brun for four days in the almonry yard after the household has taken refuge there, and feeds him oats and is licked for it.

The manuscript gives two sextons, one in the bell-loft and one in the almonry yard, neither named; the sheet draws a single man, the sexton of the bell-loft, and gives him the panel An almonry sexton feeds oats to an ox, so that the page joins them and does not claim the manuscript does. The sheet's fifty-seven years, Frankish, from Marnholt, wiry build of about 5 ft 7 in, pale lined face, thin white hair, pale grey eyes, clean-shaven face, rope-callused palms and plain grey gown are its own, as is the counting of the strokes aloud; its expression panels run Vigilance, Counting Aloud, Weariness and Devotion.
