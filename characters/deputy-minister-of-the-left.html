The Deputy Minister of the Left

Deputy Minister of the Left · unnamed · about 52 years old by his sheet

He has one sentence in the manuscript. At the quarterly Reading of the Tally in the Hall of the Two Seals, in the third week of Averin, the Secretary of the Bureau of Inquiry, Sadamune, reads out from the Book of the Ordeal the record of nine accused in a little over eleven years: five confessed and were beheaded at the Southern Gate; four put out a hand, and the Stone took four. “Four for four,” says the Deputy Minister of the Left, with real pleasure, and the particular warmth that a room gives to a figure it has been hoping for goes down both rows of the dais. It is a very good figure, the best that any law in the history of the realm has produced, and it requires no explanation; and it is, as the Secretary has known for a fortnight, the only figure in the Book that nobody has thought to put a denominator under.

That is the whole of him. The manuscript gives him no name, age or appearance, does not say what he does in the Ministry beyond his title, and does not follow him afterward; the Left Ministry falls in the Rising and nothing is said of its deputy. The pleasure is enough to place him: it is the room's pleasure, and it is what the Secretary has to stand up against, courteously answered by Sukenori and entered beneath the figure as “a procedural observation, not pursued.” The sheet fills in a plump, pale, lightly powdered man of fifty-two with a neat topknot under a lacquered cap, a thin moustache, a rich purple robe, a folding fan and a ring on every second finger. All of that is the sheet's own.
