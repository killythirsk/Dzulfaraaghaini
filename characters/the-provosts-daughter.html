The Provost's Daughter

Daughter of Ottavio Brandi · unnamed · died on the ninth of Garren, aged 16

She is seven when her father puts her hand into her aunt's in the cathedral at Montclar and says, in the voice he uses for ledgers, that the Order's work will take him some years, which in the Order's practice means the circuits of the provincial houses and no more than the three weeks of each Reckoning-tide at home. She says, quite calmly, that she will write. She writes once a week for nine years, in a round clear child's hand that grows with the years into a young woman's, and he answers every letter on the day he opens it, in a hand that does not change, beginning with the weather he is not in. He has never once missed; the manuscript calls it the only discipline of his life that he was not taught. In the autumn he remarks to the brazier that the hand in her latest letters has grown a little steadier than a girl of sixteen might be expected to make it, and decides, as a man decides who has been cold for a very long time and does not wish to learn why, that a daughter is permitted to improve.

She never appears in the manuscript alive. She dies of a fever of three days on the ninth of Garren, not painfully; on the second night she asks for her father once, and does not ask again. Her letter of the sixth is still unsealed in the drawer of her desk, because there was no time, and her aunt, who knows what the news would do to him, seals it afterward and sends it, and writes every one that follows in the child's hand and the child's voice. The case learns what the letters were on the twentieth of Iverin, when Ottavio Brandi reads the second line of his sister's letter. He goes on writing to her, weekly, beginning with the weather, and keeps the letters unsealed in a drawer; the Examiners who clear his desk find five hundred and seventy-two of them. The manuscript gives her age and her handwriting. The sheet's slim five-foot-three, narrow serious face with her father's high forehead, two dark braids, pale grey eyes, ink-stained fingertips and grey-blue wool dress with a white collar are its own.
