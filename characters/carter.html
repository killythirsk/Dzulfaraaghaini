Carter

A carter of the Aure road · patron of the Three Sheaves · 38 years old

A broad, red, genial, gap-toothed carter of thirty-eight from Marnholt in Austrenne, about 5 ft 9 in, with matted brown hair, brown eyes, a thick brown beard, weathered skin, a whip-scar across his left hand, a mud-spattered wool tunic, leather gaiters and a short carter's whip at his belt, a regular at Waldrada's Three Sheaves. The other pages give him one phrase: in the first winter of the black salt a carter has called the powder the Widow's Ashes (Carter's Wife, Old Sot by the Fire), and the sheet's large scene is captioned He names it “the Widow's Ashes”. Whether the carter's wife whose page is on file is his wife, the sheets do not say. The publication notes add that the manuscript may mean one carter or two, one who names the powder and one who, drunk and generous, says that the secret is probably the dust and is laughed at; the notes merge them into a single drinker, and so does this page.

Beside that scene the sheet gives him a second line: He suggests the secret is probably “the dust” while others laugh. It draws him among laughing men beside a loaded cart and its horse, a mug in one hand and the other arm thrown out; a smaller scene has him leaning back against a cart, laughing, with the mug raised. His expression panels run Genial, Drunk and Generous, Dismissed by Laughter and Road-weary. The carter on the sheet is a man of the Aure road, and the sheet names the Three Sheaves as his tavern; the journeyman cooper, who calls the powder a splinter of Solan's fire, is the other voice of that room whose phrase survives.

Written from the sheet, from what the other Case 4181 pages already say, and from the character notes in the case's publication-prep file; the manuscript itself was not to hand. His age of 38, broad build of about 5 ft 9 in, ruddy weathered skin, matted brown hair, brown eyes and mud-brown wool with dark leather are the sheet's own.
