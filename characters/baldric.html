Baldric

Steward of Hartenmark's granary office · liked far more than he was ever audited · mid-fifties by his sheet

The steward of Hartenmark's granary office, whom everyone at court has liked considerably more than they have ever bothered to audit. In her fourth year there Hildegund learns to read a ledger the way her blood once read a burning corridor, for the number that does not sit quite right beside its neighbours, and uncovers a decade of quiet embezzlement. Reinolt, who spent two winters early in their exile sparring in the same practice yard as Baldric's own son, asks her the night before she lays it before the council whether she means to ruin the father quietly or publicly. She chooses public, on the ground that a public ruin teaches the rest of the granary office a lesson a quiet one cannot, and learns afterwards, from Reinolt's silence, that competence and decency are not entries in the same ledger. Baldric's eldest son leaves Hartenmark's service within the month and does not, in nine further years, fully return, a cost that appears on no account she keeps and that she never forgets.

The manuscript does not say how his ruin was carried out. The sheet's mid-fifties, grey thinning hair, round genial face and moustache, good unostentatious clerk's robes and ledger under his arm are its own, with a second panel of him kneeling between guards.
