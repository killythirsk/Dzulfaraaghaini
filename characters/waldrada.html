Waldrada

Widow, keeper of the Three Sheaves · 54 years old by her sheet

The stout, cheerful widow who keeps the Three Sheaves, the tavern at the corner of Tanners' Lane and the Cooper's Row in the Middle Town, under a sign of three wheat-ears bound with red cord. She waters the beer one part to five and is held in consequence to be the most honest woman in Marnholt; her sheet gives the ratio and the reputation as captions. Her room is where the black salt acquires its names, in the voices of a barge-hand, a carter, a carter's wife, an old sot by the fire, a journeyman cooper and a drover from the north, and where, on the night King Theudebald dies, a boatman says he died of arithmetic and she says that a king who counts twice dies once. Her nephew, an eel-seller, carries the tavern's news down to the ox-stall of the Low Forge, and a barmaid of her house, the Girl Behind the Bar, listens to it all.

Her pull-quote, They are all buying something, is the sheet's own line, not one the other pages give her. Written from the sheet, from what the other Case 4181 pages already say, and from the character notes in the case's publication-prep file; the manuscript itself was not to hand. Her age of 54, her stout build of about 5 ft 4 in, round flushed face, greying red hair tied beneath a cap, brown eyes and ruddy complexion, and her pewter mug and wiping cloth are the sheet's own, as are its expression panels, Cheer, Knowing Smile, At Service and Widow's Quiet; its scene shows her behind her bar with a mug in one hand and the cloth in the other.

“They are all buying something.”
